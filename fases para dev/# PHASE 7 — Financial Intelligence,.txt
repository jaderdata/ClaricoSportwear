# PHASE 7 — Financial Intelligence, Profitability & Analytics Dashboard

## Project Context

Phases 1 through 6 established the operational foundation of the commerce platform.

### Phase 1 — Catalog Migration

Existing marketplace listings were extracted and migrated.

### Phase 2 — Central Product Database

Supabase became the central source of truth for product information.

### Phase 3 — Product Management

A private admin panel was created to manage products, images, variants, pricing, tags, and sales-channel selection.

### Phase 4 — Marketplace Publishing

Products can be published to selected marketplaces.

### Phase 5 — Synchronization & Reconciliation

Marketplace listings can be monitored, updated, reconciled, and verified.

### Phase 6 — Orders, Inventory & Fulfillment

Orders from multiple channels can be normalized into Supabase, connected to products, tracked through production and fulfillment, and reflected in inventory.

Phase 7 adds the financial and analytical layer.

The system should now answer not only:

> What sold?

but also:

> Was that sale actually profitable?

and:

> Which products, channels, designs, customers, and strategies are producing the best business results?

---

# MAIN OBJECTIVE

Build a centralized **Financial Intelligence & Analytics System** inside the existing admin platform.

The system should transform raw operational data into actionable business information.

It should eventually provide clear answers to questions such as:

* How much revenue did I generate?
* How much profit did I generate?
* What is my gross margin?
* What is my net margin?
* Which marketplace is most profitable?
* Which marketplace generates the most revenue?
* Which product sells the most?
* Which product is the most profitable?
* Which designs are growing?
* Which products are underperforming?
* How much do marketplace fees cost me?
* How much do shipping costs reduce margin?
* How much does each shirt actually cost me?
* What is the average order value?
* How many units am I selling per day?
* Which channels deserve more attention?
* Which products should I stop producing?
* Which products should I scale?

# STEP 2 — Define Financial Terminology

Before building dashboards, define the exact meaning of each metric.

For example:

## Gross Merchandise Value / Gross Sales

Total merchandise value before deductions.

## Discounts

Discounts applied to sales.

## Refunds

Amounts returned to customers.

## Net Sales

Gross sales minus discounts and refunds.

## Marketplace Fees

Fees charged by Depop, Vinted, eBay, or other channels.

## Payment Processing Fees

Payment-provider transaction fees.

## Cost of Goods Sold

Direct product cost associated with sold items.

## Gross Profit

Net sales minus direct product cost.

## Contribution Profit

Gross profit minus marketplace fees, payment fees, shipping subsidy, packaging, and other variable selling costs.

## Contribution Margin

Contribution profit divided by net sales.

These definitions must be documented and used consistently.

# STEP 4 — Product Cost History

Costs change over time.

Do not assume the current cost of a shirt was the same cost when an old order was fulfilled.

Use historical cost snapshots or effective-date cost records.

Example:

January blank shirt cost:

`$3.50`

August blank shirt cost:

`$4.20`

An order from January should retain the January economics.

Historical profitability must not change every time current supplier costs change.

# STEP 7 — Fee Estimation

Sometimes exact marketplace fees may not be available immediately.

If estimated values are needed, clearly distinguish:

`estimated`

from:

`confirmed`

Example:

`marketplace_fee_status = estimated`

Later, once the actual transaction data becomes available, replace or reconcile the estimate.

Never present estimates as confirmed facts.

---

# STEP 8 — Shipping Economics

Separate:

**Shipping charged to customer**

from:

**Actual shipping cost**

These are not the same thing.

Example:

Customer shipping charge:

`$6.00`

Actual label cost:

`$5.10`

Shipping contribution:

`+$0.90`

Another order:

Customer shipping:

`$0`

Actual shipping cost:

`$5.10`

Shipping contribution:

`-$5.10`

This difference materially affects profitability.

* Equipment lease
* Website hosting
* Storage

# STEP 16 — Margin Levels

## Contribution Margin

Do not hide the distinction.

Different decisions require different margin levels.

# CORE PRINCIPLE

Phase 6 answers:

**"What did I sell and what do I need to fulfill?"**

Phase 7 answers:

**"Did I actually make money, where did I make it, and what should I do more or less of?"**

The objective is not to create a pretty dashboard.

The objective is to create a **decision system** that helps the business allocate time, capital, inventory, and attention intelligently.

The final goal is:

**One source of truth.**

**One operational system.**

**One financial view.**

**One clear picture of what is actually driving profit.**
